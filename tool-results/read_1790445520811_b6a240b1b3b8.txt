    28→## 1. Why the prior team-size assumption invalidates more than the schedule
    29→
    30→Both predecessor documents sized 6-week bets and (in v2's case) a 3-way parallel Bet 6/7/8 stretch on
    31→collaborative throughput from three engineers. With one, two things are true simultaneously:
    32→
    33→- **All engineering parallelism is void, not risky.** Sequential, no exceptions.
    34→- **The per-bet week estimates aren't just "3× longer" — they're wrong in kind, not just magnitude.**
    35→  AI agents (Cline, Jules, Kilo) multiply *implementation* throughput; they don't substitute for the
    36→  *review and judgment* capacity three people provided. One person's attention still gates every merge.
    37→  That bottleneck doesn't scale down linearly with headcount, so no simple division of the old estimates
    38→  produces a trustworthy number.
    39→
    40→Marketer and media are real, non-blocking capacity — just not engineering capacity, and not usable
    41→against the critical path without the fix in §3.
    42→
    43→**Conclusion: no timeline in this document is trustworthy until Milestone 0 (§4) produces a measured
    44→number. Anywhere a week-count appears below, treat it as a placeholder, not a commitment.**
    45→
    46→## 2. Methodology shift: Spiral Deepening replaces Radial Incremental
    47→
    48→The prior model was **horizontal completion of a vertical ring**: finish Core, then Hub, then Spokes.
    49→That's viable for a team that can absorb a late integration surprise. Solo, a surprise at week 30 (the
    50→first time two rings actually meet) is close to fatal — there's no one else to reprioritize onto it.
    51→
    52→The new model is **vertical deepening of a horizontal slice**:
    53→
    54→- **Milestone 0** builds a genuine end-to-end Pulse — Exemplar One, made real — touching all six rings
    55→  at minimal depth (roughly 10% of Core's eventual surface, one Hub service, one Bridge stub, one
    56→  Internal Spoke, one External Spoke). This is the walking skeleton.
    57→- **Every subsequent bet deepens one ring's slice toward production quality**, informed by what
    58→  Milestone 0 already proved works end-to-end.
    59→- The bet map is therefore a **matrix — blueprints × depth level — not a linear sequence.** A given
    60→  blueprint may be touched across several non-adjacent bets as it deepens.
    61→
    62→### 2.1 Interface freeze vs. implementation depth — the gap this creates, closed
    63→
    64→Spiral Deepening has one failure mode the original Ring Lock didn't: if a blueprint gets revisited
    65→across many bets as it deepens, every dependent could be building against a moving target the whole
    66→project, which is strictly worse than Ring Lock's original guarantee ("this interface is stable now").
    67→**Fix: separate the two things Ring Lock used to bundle together.**
    68→
    69→- **Interface freeze** — a blueprint's public contract (its `interface`s, its DTO shapes, its declared
    70→  dependencies) freezes the first time it's implemented at *any* depth, including Milestone 0's minimal
    71→  version. This is a one-time event per blueprint, early, cheap to get right because the surface is small.
    72→- **Implementation depth** — the internal logic behind that frozen interface can deepen across many later
    73→  bets without requiring a new freeze, *as long as the public contract doesn't change.*
    74→- **Changing an already-frozen interface is an ADR-gated event**, same discipline as the old Ring Lock
    75→  violation — not a casual edit, regardless of which bet is "supposed" to own that blueprint.
    76→
    77→This is what makes the matrix model safe: dependents build against a contract that's stable from
    78→Milestone 0 forward, even while the implementation behind it keeps deepening.

    80→## 3. Cooldown 0 — before Milestone 0, not optional
    81→
    82→Marketer and media working "unblocked" through `HUB-13` and `HUB-26` is only true for the *plumbing*.
    83→Content written against an imagined UI gets thrown away when the real layout ships. **Cooldown 0**
    84→happens before Milestone 0 starts and freezes three things:
    85→
    86→1. **`ESPOKE-05`'s UI wireframe** — marketer writes copy against this, not the eventual implementation.
    87→2. **`HUB-26`'s theme token contract** — media produces assets that fit these slots, not guessed
    88→   dimensions.
    89→3. **`HUB-13`'s string key taxonomy** — marketer writes strings against real keys from day one, not a
    90→   retrofit pass later.
    91→
    92→**Change control for Cooldown-0 artifacts:** these three freeze under the same ADR-gate discipline as a
    93→Core interface change (§2.1) — not because they're architecturally risky, but because the entire point
    94→of Cooldown 0 is giving marketer/media a stable target. Letting them drift casually reintroduces the
    95→exact rework problem Cooldown 0 exists to prevent, just moved from "engineering vs. content" to
    96→"content vs. re-content."
    97→
    98→**Duration: variable, matching §7's model — not a special unstated one-off, and not exempt from a real
    99→review cycle just because it's a one-time event.** Freezing three artifacts with a genuine marketer/media
   100→review behind each isn't faster than an ordinary cooldown, and unlike engineering's own cooldown tasks,
   101→marketer/media review isn't AI-throughput-compressible — it's still bounded by two humans' actual
   102→availability. Cooldown 0 closes when the three artifacts are genuinely frozen (reviewed, not just drafted)
   103→**and** marketer/media confirm they're unblocked on a stable target — the same "recorded confirmation, not
   104→an unstated default" discipline §7 now requires, applied to the people it's actually gating this time.
   105→
   106→With Cooldown 0, marketer and media genuinely start unblocked in week 1. Without it, "non-blocking
   107→track" is true in name only.
   108→
   109→**A content Ring Lock, not just a content freeze.** Cooldown 0 freezes the *contracts* content is written
   110→against; it doesn't by itself catch drift in the *content* over time. Every subsequent cooldown (§7)
   111→includes one marketer/media review checkpoint — content checked against the current state of its frozen
   112→contracts, same cadence as engineering's own cooldown reconciliation. Without this, content drift gets
   113→discovered at integration instead of at a cooldown, which is exactly the failure mode Cooldown 0 exists
   114→to prevent, just deferred to a later point in the project instead of eliminated.

   138→### 4.1 Depth scale
   139→
   140→| Depth | Meaning |
   141→|---|---|
   142→| 1 | Stub — interface exists, no real logic |
   143→| 2 | Happy path — the intended case works end-to-end |
   144→| 3 | Error paths — failure modes handled, not just the happy case |
   145→| 4 | Observability — logging, metrics, audit wired per its blueprint's CI criteria |
   146→| 5 | Production hardening — stated performance targets met, security-reviewed |
   147→| 6 | At-scale verified — load-tested against real, not assumed, targets |
   148→
   149→"Production depth" (§2.1) means depth 5 unless a blueprint's own document states otherwise. The bet
   150→matrix (§2) has this scale as its second axis.
   151→
   152→> **Two distinct scales — do not confuse them.** The *blueprint-maturity* scale above (1=stub … 6=at-scale)
   153→> is a property of each blueprint's implementation. The *Pulse entry-radius* scale (1=Outer Rim … 6=Core)
   154→> is a property of where a runtime Pulse enters the Wheel (see `PULSE-MODEL.md`). They use overlapping
   155→> numbers by coincidence; they measure different things.
   156→
   157→### 4.2 After Milestone 0 lands — the missing chapter
   158→
   159→§5 describes the *measurement*; this describes what happens with it.
   160→
   161→**Bet duration — stated, not left open, with the unit mismatch resolved for Bet 1 specifically.** A bet's
   162→time-box is the throughput-derived estimate for its scoped work (§5), with a 1.5× ceiling as its own kill
   163→trigger — except Bet 1, where that formula can't actually be computed: §5's throughput is a *build* rate
   164→(depth 0→2), and Bet 1 is *deepening* work (depth 2→3+), the exact unit mismatch §5/§9 warn about. **Bet 1's
   165→ceiling uses `W` (Milestone 0's measured duration) directly, at 1.5×W** — not because build and deepening
   166→throughput are assumed equal, but because `W` is the best available reference point before any real
   167→deepening data exists. Bet 1's actual duration becomes the first true deepening-throughput baseline, and
   168→Bet 2 onward derive their ceilings from that instead of from `W`.
   169→
   170→**Bet scoping rule — per-blueprint, matching §4.3's floor, not a single ring-wide depth.** Pick the ring
   171→with the lowest average depth across its currently-touched blueprints, and deepen its below-floor
   172→blueprints to their own per-blueprint targets (§4.3) — there is no single shared "ring depth" to name,
   173→since each blueprint's target depends on its own admission point. Concretely: if Milestone 0 leaves
   174→`CORE-02` at depth 2 and `CORE-04`/`05`/`06` at depth 1 (illustrative — exact Milestone-0 exit depths per
   175→component aren't pinned down until it actually runs), Bet 1 is "Core's below-floor blueprints to their
   176→per-blueprint targets" — `CORE-02` to 3, `CORE-04`/`05`/`06` to 2 — not the single number "Core to depth 3"
   177→a global floor would have implied.
   178→
   179→### 4.3 Lap structure — widens and deepens every cycle, not deepen-then-widen
   180→
   181→The prior draft of this document defined three one-shot phases that deepened Milestone 0's original 8
   182→blueprints to depth 5 *before* admitting a 9th. An external review caught this correctly: that's not
   183→Spiral Deepening, it's Radial Incremental at smaller scale — the same late-integration-surprise problem
   184→this document exists to prevent, just relocated from "ring boundaries" to "blueprint #9." The fix proposed
   185→alongside that catch (widen at two of three phase exits) was a real improvement but only covered a handful
   186→of blueprints through three named phases — it can't reach 96 blueprints without repeating. So: laps, not
   187→phases, and each lap does both things every time.
   188→
   189→**Lap k, for k = 1, 2, 3…:**
   190→
   191→1. **Widen.** For each ring that still has unadmitted blueprints, admit its next-most-depended-upon
   192→   not-yet-touched blueprint at depth 1. (Once a ring is fully widened, it contributes no admission that
   193→   lap — the rule has nothing left to act on for it, which is expected, not an error.) "Next-most-depended-upon"
   194→   is decided against the current matrix's real dependency edges (`INDEX.md` §5.2 — verified to be an
   195→   actual Mermaid graph with real `A --> B` edges, not prose; see the coverage caveat under gap C below),
   196→   not arbitrarily — e.g., once `HUB-01` is in, the next Hub admission is whichever other Hub blueprint the
   197→   already-touched Bridge/Spoke set most needs (`HUB-02` Cache and `HUB-04` Identity are the likely first
   198→   candidates, since `BRIDGE-01`/`ISPOKE-09`/`ESPOKE-01` all reference them even at low depth — both
   199→   confirmed present with real edges in `§5.2`'s current coverage).
   200→
   201→   **Widen is need-driven, not mandatory-per-ring (v3.5 addition, formalizing actual Lap 1/Lap 2 practice).**
   202→   "Admit its next-most-depended-upon not-yet-touched blueprint" presumes a ring's remaining blueprints are
   203→   actually pulled on by something already in the matrix. When reconciled `INDEX.md` §5.2 evidence shows
   204→   *nothing currently admitted* depends on any of a ring's remaining blueprints yet, that ring's widen step
   205→   for the lap is **skipped, not forced** — but the skip must be **explicitly logged with its reasoning** in
   206→   the lap's entry-gate record (e.g., Lap 2's entry gate logging `CORE-15 (not needed)` alongside what *was*
   207→   admitted), not silently omitted. This keeps the rule's intent (don't widen ahead of real dependency pull)
   208→   without quietly drifting into "some rings just stop getting widened and nobody decided that." A ring
   209→   skipped this way remains eligible for the very next lap where the graph shows a real pull on it.
   210→
   211→   **OD-02 and lap-1 widening — checked against the actual `CORE-16` blueprint, not assumed (v3.4(3)
   212→   correction).** `CORE-16` and `HUB-04` are both plausible early widening candidates (real graph edges into
   213→   `BRIDGE-01`). An earlier draft (v3.4/v3.4(2)) excluded them from lap-1 widening to avoid ADR-thrash on
   214→   freshly-frozen interfaces if OD-02 (post-quantum JWT) were still open. **v3.4(3) re-verified directly
   215→   and DROPPED that exclusion:** `CORE-16`'s actual interface (`EncrypterInterface`) doesn't expose its cipher
   216→   choice in its method signatures — algorithm detail is internal, and the blueprint states plainly
   217→   `CORE-16` "ships no asymmetric primitive" at all; JWT signing lives in `HUB-04` per `ADR-003` (ES256), not
   218→   in `CORE-16`. `HUB-04`'s own public contract (`attempt()`, `login()`, `check()`, …) is similarly
   219→   high-level, with the signing algorithm expected to live inside an internal `TokenService`, not the frozen
   220→   interface. **Expectation, not a guarantee: OD-02 resolves as an internal implementation change under an
   221→   already-frozen `HUB-04` interface, not an interface change.** No lap-1 exclusion remains — constraining
   222→   widening for a risk verification suggests probably won't materialize would be its own instance of planning
   223→   against an assumption instead of a check. If OD-02's actual resolution turns out to need a `HUB-04`
   224→   interface change anyway, that's an ADR-gated change like any other (§2.1) — expensive, but not a scenario
   225→   this document failed to anticipate.
   226→
   227→2. **Deepen — per-blueprint floor, not a global lap floor.** Each blueprint's target floor is *its own
   228→   admission depth plus the number of laps completed since its own admission*, capped at depth 5 — not a
   229→   single floor number shared by every blueprint regardless of when it entered the matrix.
   230→
   231→**Why per-blueprint, not the global "lap 1→3, lap 2→4, lap 3→5" schedule stated in an earlier draft:** an
   232→external review correctly caught that the global version contradicts itself — a blueprint widened into the
   233→matrix during lap 2's widen step enters at depth 1, and lap 2's own deepen step would then require it to
   234→reach depth 4 in the same lap: three depth levels in one bet-cycle, alongside a full widen pass across all
   235→6 rings, for something that had zero prior laps to build in. The fix reviewed alongside that catch (exempt a
   236→blueprint from the floor for its admission lap only, deepening starts next lap) is directionally right but
   237→only *defers* the cliff by one lap rather than removing it — a blueprint admitted in lap 2 would still owe a
   238→4-level jump (1→5) by lap 3 under that version. The per-blueprint relative floor removes the cliff entirely:
   239→every blueprint deepens at the same steady +1-level-per-lap pace starting from its own admission, regardless
   240→of the global lap number, so nothing is ever asked to catch up faster than anything else did. Applied to the
   241→original 8: Milestone 0 gets them to depth 1–2 (their "lap 0"), lap 1 brings them to depth 3, lap 2 to depth
   242→4, lap 3 to depth 5 — which reproduces the original schedule's intent for the blueprints it was actually
   243→designed around, while extending consistently to every later admission instead of breaking on them.
   244→
   245→**Termination:** the lap structure ends when all 96 blueprints are at depth 5 and the matrix has fully
   246→widened — not at a fixed lap count, since how many laps that takes depends on how fast widening outpaces
   247→deepening, which isn't known until real laps run (see §5's throughput-unit caveat).
   248→
   249→This keeps the matrix genuinely two-dimensional throughout — width and depth grow together every cycle, at a
   250→uniform per-blueprint pace — rather than fully deepening a fixed subset before ever widening, or asking
   251→newly-widened blueprints to deepen faster than anything else did.
   252→
   253→**Gap A — lap ↔ bet relationship, stated:** a lap is not one atomic bet. It is one widen pass (up to 6
   254→ring-admissions) plus up to 6 deepening bets — one per ring that currently has a blueprint below its own
   255→per-blueprint floor (§4.2 scopes each such bet to a single named ring and target). Not every ring
   256→necessarily needs a deepening bet in every lap; a ring with nothing below floor that lap contributes only
   257→its widen-step admission.
   258→
   259→**Gap B — bet kill semantics, stated:** hitting the 1.5× ceiling (§4.2) means: stop the bet, keep whatever
   260→depth was actually reached — no forced revert, since the interface was already frozen at admission (§2.1), so
   261→partial deepening progress is never wasted. Re-scope explicitly: either the same ring gets the next
   262→deepening bet-slot with adjusted expectations, or the architecture lead defers that ring's remaining
   263→deepening to a later lap. This is a routine re-scoping event, not Milestone 0's "stop and reassess the whole
   264→model" — killing one bet doesn't imply the methodology itself is wrong, the way Milestone 0 exceeding its own
   265→ceiling would.
   266→
   267→**Gap C — is `INDEX.md` §5.2 actually queryable, checked against the live repo, not assumed:** yes, and more
   268→precisely than "yes/no" — it's a real Mermaid graph with genuine `A --> B` edges, but its current coverage is
   269→37 of 96 blueprints (all 20 Core, 10 Hub components explicitly marked "selected critical," `BRIDGE-01`, the 2
   270→exemplar Spokes `ISPOKE-01`/`ESPOKE-01`, and all 4 Deploy). `HUB-02` and `HUB-04` — this document's own named
   271→lap-1 widening candidates — are both in that covered set with real edges, so early laps have genuine data to
   272→widen against, not the arbitrary fallback gap C worried about. The real constraint: once widening needs a
   273→blueprint outside that 37-node coverage (most of `HUB-11`–`30` beyond the 10 already included, and every
   274→`ISPOKE`/`ESPOKE` beyond the 2 exemplars), `§5.2` has no edge data for it and the "next-most-depended-upon"
   275→rule has nothing to compute against. This isn't a lap-1 blocker — it's a known, scoped point where `§5.2`'s
   276→coverage needs extending, roughly once widening exhausts the 37-node set already covered. Tracked in §9, not
   277→treated as resolved.

   279→## 5. Calibration — formula, not a vibes-check
   280→
   281→```
   282→N  = blueprints in Milestone 0 = 8 (corrected — see §4)
   283→W  = weeks Milestone 0 actually took (measured, not estimated)
   284→throughput = N / W                    (blueprints/week)
   285→remaining  = 96 − N = 88 blueprints
   286→projected  = remaining / throughput = 11W   (weeks, bet-work only, excludes cooldowns)
   287→```
   288→
   289→Example: Milestone 0 takes 4 weeks → 44 weeks of remaining bet-work. Takes 6 weeks → 66 weeks.
   290→
   291→**Caveat this projection doesn't state on its own: `throughput` here is a *build* rate (depth 0→2, what
   292→Milestone 0 measures), applied to 88 blueprints that also need *deepening* passes (2→3→4→5), which is very
   293→likely a different unit, not just a smaller version of the same one.** Deepening could be faster than initial
   294→build — interfaces are already frozen (§2.1), patterns repeat within a ring — or slower, since depth 4–5 work
   295→(observability, hardening, security review) is often genuinely harder than getting a happy path working. The
   296→11W figure is therefore a **build-only estimate**, not a full project estimate, and should be read as such
   297→until real lap data (§4.3) produces a separate deepening throughput to add to it. This is tracked as an
   298→explicit unknown in §9, not resolved here by assertion.
   299→
   300→**On the "40–70 week" range stated in an earlier draft of this document: it was wrong, and re-deriving it
   301→properly matters, not just re-labeling it.** It was built on an uncorrected blueprint count, and a review of
   302→this document correctly flagged that the arithmetic didn't actually derive it from the stated formula.
   303→Re-running that same formula with the *correct* N=8 doesn't produce a lower, tighter number — it produces a
   304→*higher* one, because a smaller Milestone 0 measured over the same W implies lower throughput. At W=4–6:
   305→44–66 weeks of build-only bet-work (see caveat above), before cooldowns (§7, now variable per cooldown,
   306→gated on a recorded rest check rather than a fixed number) and before Cooldown 0 (§3, same variable model).
   307→Neither cooldown duration nor cooldown *count* is a number this document will manufacture — under
   308→the lap model, bet count isn't fixed in advance the way it was in the linear model, so a precise
   309→cooldown-overhead figure would be exactly the kind of false precision this section exists to avoid. Treat
   310→total elapsed time as **Milestone 0 (W) + Cooldown 0 (2wk) + build-work (11W) + deepening-work (unknown until
   311→lap 1) + cooldown overhead (unknown until a real bet cadence exists)** — not as a single number, and not until
   312→laps actually produce one.
   313→
   314→**Recalibrate throughput after every lap, not once.** Build throughput and deepening throughput are separate
   315→numbers (see caveat above), and within-ring pattern reuse will likely change both as the project goes on.
   316→Only the *current* projection, refreshed at every lap boundary, is ever trusted.
   317→
   318→**Until Milestone 0 completes and lap 1 establishes cadence:** no total is stated as fact anywhere in this
   319→document, including here.

   321→## 6. The linter is the second reviewer, not the notary
   322→
   323→With one engineer, `Verification/lint/run.php` is the only thing that catches what a second reviewer would
   324→have. Scope expands accordingly — and this expansion is itself real engineering work, budgeted explicitly
   325→rather than left to compete silently with blueprint deepening: **lint-scope expansion happens during cooldown
   326→weeks (§7), never during bet weeks.** If it doesn't fit in a cooldown, it carries to the next one rather than
   327→eating into bet time.
   328→
   329→**Not live during Milestone 0 itself.** Cooldown 0 (§3) is scoped to content contracts, not engineering lint
   330→— so Milestone 0 runs against the *existing* lint scope only. The expanded checks below become active
   331→starting with the first post-Milestone-0 cooldown. Don't assume the expanded scope is protecting Milestone 0's
   332→own build.
   333→
   334→| Check | Status |
   335→|---|---|
   336→| Cross-reference validity against `INDEX.md` (existing) | Already load-bearing |
   337→| Pulse 6-tuple consistency across repos | Promote from nice-to-have to required — this is now the only thing catching Structure-doc drift |
   338→| Naming drift, folder vs. `INDEX.md` (OD-05) | Promote from "docs cleanup" to lint-enforced, not manually tracked |
   339→| Soft-Freeze violations — a PR touching a frozen interface (§2.1) without a citing ADR | Auto-reject at CI, not a review comment |
   340→| Blueprint-fidelity drift — does the implementation still match its `STRUCTURE-XX` spec | **Honest caveat, not oversold:** full semantic diffing of code against prose spec isn't a solved CI problem. Start as a lighter-weight periodic check (a scripted structural diff — class/interface names present, method signatures match — not full behavioral equivalence) rather than claiming this is a complete automated gate from day one. Tighten it as patterns emerge from real drift instances. |
   341→

   343→exactly the kind of unverified assertion this whole audit exists to catch, just relocated into the process
   344→document instead of the architecture docs.
   345→

   347→> performs exactly **three** checks — (1) reference targets exist / are in-range, (2) misattribution-phrase

   352→## 7. Cooldowns: variable duration, gated on a recorded rest check — not a fixed 2 weeks (v3.5)
   353→
   354→A 3-person team's 1-week cooldown gave each person ~2.3 days of catch-up. Solo, cooldown has to cover doc
   355→reconciliation, OD triage, refactor backlog, the review backlog that accumulated during the bet, *and*
   356→recovery time nobody else provides by covering for you. Solo burnout carries higher risk than team burnout
   357→precisely because there's no redundancy to absorb it.
   358→
   359→**What changed from the prior fixed-2-week rule, and why:** AI-agent-augmented throughput makes cooldown's
   360→*task list* (worklog reconciliation, ADR/discrepancy resolution, lint expansion, recalibration) completable in
   361→hours, not weeks — verified directly against this project's own history: Cooldown 1 ran its full task list in
   362→~14 hours, and the following lap's own cadence matched. A fixed 2-week clock measured against task completion
   363→was never actually the safeguard §7 needed; it was a proxy for one, and the proxy broke the moment task
   364→throughput stopped resembling a human's. **The actual requirement was never "2 weeks have elapsed" — it was
   365→"the operator is not running on unaddressed burnout debt."** Keeping a fixed duration after that proxy broke
   366→would either force artificial idleness (tasks done, nothing left to do, calendar still ticking) or — the
   367→failure mode actually observed — get silently ignored in practice because the fixed number stopped mapping to
   368→anything real.
   369→
   370→**The rule now:** cooldown duration is **variable**, bounded by two things instead of a clock:
   371→1. **The task list (§5.3/§5.4/Cooldown-1-style work) must actually complete** — reconciliation, discrepancy
   372→   resolution, lint/fitness expansion, recalibration. This floor doesn't move.
   373→2. **Cooldown does not close until the operator explicitly records a rest/decompression check** — not a task
   374→   checkbox, a genuine self-report, written into the cooldown-closing record alongside the task-completion
   375→   summary: *did real decompression happen, separate from whether the tasks finished fast.* "Tasks are done"
   376→   and "I am not running on burnout debt" are different claims, and only the second one is what this section
   377→   was ever actually protecting. If the honest answer is no, cooldown continues — regardless of how fast the
   378→   task list finished — until the answer is yes.
   379→
   380→This is deliberately not self-certifying by omission: the check must be a stated yes, not an unstated default.
   381→A cooldown record with the task list complete and no rest-check statement is an incomplete cooldown, not a
   382→short one.

   416→## 9. Explicit unknowns — do not silently resolve these
   417→
   418→- The actual Milestone 0 duration, and therefore every downstream week-count.
   419→- Whether `CORE-02`'s real build time matches the "unblocks everything" framing it's had since Finding 8, or
   420→  turns out harder than expected as the first thing built solo.
   421→- Whether blueprint-fidelity drift-checking (§6) needs to become a harder automated gate sooner than planned,
   422→  based on how much real drift Milestone 0 and the first few deepening bets actually produce.
   423→- **Whether deepening throughput (depth 2→5) differs from build throughput (depth 0→2), and by how much (§5).**
   424→  Genuinely unresolvable until lap 1 (§4.3) produces a real deepening data point — the projection in §5 is
   425→  explicitly build-only until then.
   426→- **How many laps the widen/deepen structure (§4.3) actually takes to reach full coverage.** Depends on the
   427→  relative rate of widening vs. deepening, which is itself unknown until laps 1–2 run.
   428→- **`INDEX.md` §5.2's dependency-graph coverage (37 of 96 blueprints, verified) needs extending before widening
   429→  exhausts it.** Not a lap-1 blocker — `HUB-02`/`HUB-04` are both covered — but there's no stated trigger for
   430→  *when* to extend §5.2's coverage before the widen rule runs out of real edge data to compute against.
   431→  Trigger, disambiguated: extend a ring's §5.2 coverage once 50% **of that ring's currently-covered set** has
   432→  been admitted into the matrix (e.g., Hub's covered set is 10 of its 30 blueprints — extend once 5 of those 10
   433→  are admitted, not once 15 of all 30 are), since that's the point where the widen rule is about to run out of
   434→  real edges to choose from for that ring, which is the actual failure mode this trigger exists to prevent.
   435→
   436→Per Governance Rule 9 (this session's own precedent): open questions get recorded, never silently resolved. The
   437→six above go in `OPEN-DECISIONS.md` as new entries once this document is ratified.
