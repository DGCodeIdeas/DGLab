    26→## 1. Why the prior team-size assumption invalidates more than the schedule
    27→
    28→Both predecessor documents sized 6-week bets and (in v2's case) a 3-way parallel Bet 6/7/8 stretch on
    29→collaborative throughput from three engineers. With one, two things are true simultaneously:
    30→
    31→- **All engineering parallelism is void, not risky.** Sequential, no exceptions.
    32→- **The per-bet week estimates aren't just "3× longer" — they're wrong in kind, not just magnitude.**
    33→  AI agents (Cline, Jules, Kilo) multiply *implementation* throughput; they don't substitute for the
    34→  *review and judgment* capacity three people provided. One person's attention still gates every merge.
    35→  That bottleneck doesn't scale down linearly with headcount, so no simple division of the old estimates
    36→  produces a trustworthy number.
    37→
    38→Marketer and media are real, non-blocking capacity — just not engineering capacity, and not usable
    39→against the critical path without the fix in §3.
    40→
    41→**Conclusion: no timeline in this document is trustworthy until Milestone 0 (§4) produces a measured
    42→number. Anywhere a week-count appears below, treat it as a placeholder, not a commitment.**
    43→
    44→## 2. Methodology shift: Spiral Deepening replaces Radial Incremental
    45→
    46→The prior model was **horizontal completion of a vertical ring**: finish Core, then Hub, then Spokes.
    47→That's viable for a team that can absorb a late integration surprise. Solo, a surprise at week 30 (the
    48→first time two rings actually meet) is close to fatal — there's no one else to reprioritize onto it.
    49→
    50→The new model is **vertical deepening of a horizontal slice**:
    51→
    52→- **Milestone 0** builds a genuine end-to-end Pulse — Exemplar One, made real — touching all six rings
    53→  at minimal depth (roughly 10% of Core's eventual surface, one Hub service, one Bridge stub, one
    54→  Internal Spoke, one External Spoke). This is the walking skeleton.
    55→- **Every subsequent bet deepens one ring's slice toward production quality**, informed by what
    56→  Milestone 0 already proved works end-to-end.
    57→- The bet map is therefore a **matrix — blueprints × depth level — not a linear sequence.** A given
    58→  blueprint may be touched across several non-adjacent bets as it deepens.
    59→
    60→### 2.1 Interface freeze vs. implementation depth — the gap this creates, closed
    61→
    62→Spiral Deepening has one failure mode the original Ring Lock didn't: if a blueprint gets revisited
    63→across many bets as it deepens, every dependent could be building against a moving target the whole
    64→project, which is strictly worse than Ring Lock's original guarantee ("this interface is stable now").
    65→**Fix: separate the two things Ring Lock used to bundle together.**
    66→
    67→- **Interface freeze** — a blueprint's public contract (its `interface`s, its DTO shapes, its declared
    68→  dependencies) freezes the first time it's implemented at *any* depth, including Milestone 0's minimal
    69→  version. This is a one-time event per blueprint, early, cheap to get right because the surface is small.
    70→- **Implementation depth** — the internal logic behind that frozen interface can deepen across many later
    71→  bets without requiring a new freeze, *as long as the public contract doesn't change.*
    72→- **Changing an already-frozen interface is an ADR-gated event**, same discipline as the old Ring Lock
    73→  violation — not a casual edit, regardless of which bet is "supposed" to own that blueprint.
    74→
    75→This is what makes the matrix model safe: dependents build against a contract that's stable from
    76→Milestone 0 forward, even while the implementation behind it keeps deepening.

    78→## 3. Cooldown 0 — before Milestone 0, not optional
    79→
    80→Marketer and media working "unblocked" through `HUB-13` and `HUB-26` is only true for the *plumbing*.
    81→Content written against an imagined UI gets thrown away when the real layout ships. **Cooldown 0**
    82→happens before Milestone 0 starts and freezes three things:
    83→
    84→1. **`ESPOKE-05`'s UI wireframe** — marketer writes copy against this, not the eventual implementation.
    85→2. **`HUB-26`'s theme token contract** — media produces assets that fit these slots, not guessed
    86→   dimensions.
    87→3. **`HUB-13`'s string key taxonomy** — marketer writes strings against real keys from day one, not a
    88→   retrofit pass later.
    89→
    90→**Change control for Cooldown-0 artifacts:** these three freeze under the same ADR-gate discipline as a
    91→Core interface change (§2.1) — not because they're architecturally risky, but because the entire point
    92→of Cooldown 0 is giving marketer/media a stable target. Letting them drift casually reintroduces the
    93→exact rework problem Cooldown 0 exists to prevent, just moved from "engineering vs. content" to
    94→"content vs. re-content."
    95→
    96→**Duration: 2 weeks — matching the general cooldown length in §7, deliberately, not as a special
    97→unstated one-off.** Freezing three artifacts with a real marketer/media review cycle behind each isn't
    98→faster than an ordinary cooldown, and giving it an unstated duration would leave Milestone 0's actual
    99→start date unbounded.
   100→
   101→With Cooldown 0, marketer and media genuinely start unblocked in week 1. Without it, "non-blocking
   102→track" is true in name only.
   103→
   104→**A content Ring Lock, not just a content freeze.** Cooldown 0 freezes the *contracts* content is written
   105→against; it doesn't by itself catch drift in the *content* over time. Every subsequent cooldown (§7)
   106→includes one marketer/media review checkpoint — content checked against the current state of its frozen
   107→contracts, same cadence as engineering's own cooldown reconciliation. Without this, content drift gets
   108→discovered at integration instead of at a cooldown, which is exactly the failure mode Cooldown 0 exists
   109→to prevent, just deferred to a later point in the project instead of eliminated.

   112→

   115→`CORE-02`, `CORE-04`/`05`/`06` stubs, one Hub service (`HUB-01`), `BRIDGE-01` stub, one Internal Spoke
   116→(`ISPOKE-09`, Codex), one External Spoke (`ESPOKE-01`, Canvas).
   117→
   118→**Depth applies here too (§4.1):** most of these targets are depth 1–2 for Milestone 0's purposes.
   119→`BRIDGE-01`, `ISPOKE-09`, and `ESPOKE-01` need at least depth 2 (happy path) — a stub alone doesn't
   120→demonstrate a genuine round trip, which is the entire point of the milestone.
   121→
   122→**Success criterion:** a real HTTP request enters at the Outer Rim, crosses the Inner Rim, resolves
   123→against the Inner Spoke, and returns — the actual synchronous-radial Pulse trace, not a diagram of it.
   124→
   125→**Kill trigger, specific to Milestone 0:** if it exceeds 8 weeks, that is not "calibrate and push
   126→forward" — it's a signal the architecture or the solo-mode assumption itself may be wrong. Stop and
   127→reassess before continuing, rather than folding an 8+-week result into §5's formula as if it were a
   128→normal data point.
   129→
   130→**This is a calibration instrument, not a feature.** Its job is producing a real number for §5, not
   131→shipping anything user-facing.

   133→### 4.1 Depth scale
   134→
   135→| Depth | Meaning |
   136→|---|---|
   137→| 1 | Stub — interface exists, no real logic |
   138→| 2 | Happy path — the intended case works end-to-end |
   139→| 3 | Error paths — failure modes handled, not just the happy case |
   140→| 4 | Observability — logging, metrics, audit wired per its blueprint's CI criteria |
   141→| 5 | Production hardening — stated performance targets met, security-reviewed |
   142→| 6 | At-scale verified — load-tested against real, not assumed, targets |
   143→
   144→"Production depth" (§2.1) means depth 5 unless a blueprint's own document states otherwise. The bet
   145→matrix (§2) has this scale as its second axis.
   146→
   147→> **Two distinct scales — do not confuse them.** The *blueprint-maturity* scale above (1=stub … 6=at-scale)
   148→> is a property of each blueprint's implementation. The *Pulse entry-radius* scale (1=Outer Rim … 6=Core)
   149→> is a property of where a runtime Pulse enters the Wheel (see `PULSE-MODEL.md`). They use overlapping
   150→> numbers by coincidence; they measure different things.
   151→
   152→### 4.2 After Milestone 0 lands — the missing chapter
   153→
   154→§5 describes the *measurement*; this describes what happens with it.
   155→
   156→**Bet duration — stated, not left open, with the unit mismatch resolved for Bet 1 specifically.** A bet's
   157→time-box is the throughput-derived estimate for its scoped work (§5), with a 1.5× ceiling as its own kill
   158→trigger — except Bet 1, where that formula can't actually be computed: §5's throughput is a *build* rate
   159→(depth 0→2), and Bet 1 is *deepening* work (depth 2→3+), the exact unit mismatch §5/§9 warn about. **Bet 1's
   160→ceiling uses `W` (Milestone 0's measured duration) directly, at 1.5×W** — not because build and deepening
   161→throughput are assumed equal, but because `W` is the best available reference point before any real
   162→deepening data exists. Bet 1's actual duration becomes the first true deepening-throughput baseline, and
   163→Bet 2 onward derive their ceilings from that instead of from `W`.
   164→
   165→**Bet scoping rule — per-blueprint, matching §4.3's floor, not a single ring-wide depth.** Pick the ring
   166→with the lowest average depth across its currently-touched blueprints, and deepen its below-floor
   167→blueprints to their own per-blueprint targets (§4.3) — there is no single shared "ring depth" to name,
   168→since each blueprint's target depends on its own admission point. Concretely: if Milestone 0 leaves
   169→`CORE-02` at depth 2 and `CORE-04`/`05`/`06` at depth 1 (illustrative — exact Milestone-0 exit depths per
   170→component aren't pinned down until it actually runs), Bet 1 is "Core's below-floor blueprints to their
   171→per-blueprint targets" — `CORE-02` to 3, `CORE-04`/`05`/`06` to 2 — not the single number "Core to depth 3"
   172→a global floor would have implied.
   173→
   174→### 4.3 Lap structure — widens and deepens every cycle, not deepen-then-widen
   175→
   176→The prior draft of this document defined three one-shot phases that deepened Milestone 0's original 8
   177→blueprints to depth 5 *before* admitting a 9th. An external review caught this correctly: that's not
   178→Spiral Deepening, it's Radial Incremental at smaller scale — the same late-integration-surprise problem
   179→this document exists to prevent, just relocated from "ring boundaries" to "blueprint #9." The fix proposed
   180→alongside that catch (widen at two of three phase exits) was a real improvement but only covered a handful
   181→of blueprints through three named phases — it can't reach 96 blueprints without repeating. So: laps, not
   182→phases, and each lap does both things every time.
   183→
   184→**Lap k, for k = 1, 2, 3…:**
   185→
   186→1. **Widen.** For each ring that still has unadmitted blueprints, admit its next-most-depended-upon
   187→   not-yet-touched blueprint at depth 1. (Once a ring is fully widened, it contributes no admission that
   188→   lap — the rule has nothing left to act on for it, which is expected, not an error.) "Next-most-depended-upon"
   189→   is decided against the current matrix's real dependency edges (`INDEX.md` §5.2 — verified to be an
   190→   actual Mermaid graph with real `A --> B` edges, not prose; see the coverage caveat under gap C below),
   191→   not arbitrarily — e.g., once `HUB-01` is in, the next Hub admission is whichever other Hub blueprint the
   192→   already-touched Bridge/Spoke set most needs (`HUB-02` Cache and `HUB-04` Identity are the likely first
   193→   candidates, since `BRIDGE-01`/`ISPOKE-09`/`ESPOKE-01` all reference them even at low depth — both
   194→   confirmed present with real edges in `§5.2`'s current coverage).
   195→
   196→   **OD-02 and lap-1 widening — checked against the actual `CORE-16` blueprint, not assumed (v3.4(3)
   197→   correction).** `CORE-16` and `HUB-04` are both plausible early widening candidates (real graph edges into
   198→   `BRIDGE-01`). An earlier draft (v3.4/v3.4(2)) excluded them from lap-1 widening to avoid ADR-thrash on
   199→   freshly-frozen interfaces if OD-02 (post-quantum JWT) were still open. **v3.4(3) re-verified directly
   200→   and DROPPED that exclusion:** `CORE-16`'s actual interface (`EncrypterInterface`) doesn't expose its cipher
   201→   choice in its method signatures — algorithm detail is internal, and the blueprint states plainly
   202→   `CORE-16` "ships no asymmetric primitive" at all; JWT signing lives in `HUB-04` per `ADR-003` (ES256), not
   203→   in `CORE-16`. `HUB-04`'s own public contract (`attempt()`, `login()`, `check()`, …) is similarly
   204→   high-level, with the signing algorithm expected to live inside an internal `TokenService`, not the frozen
   205→   interface. **Expectation, not a guarantee: OD-02 resolves as an internal implementation change under an
   206→   already-frozen `HUB-04` interface, not an interface change.** No lap-1 exclusion remains — constraining
   207→   widening for a risk verification suggests probably won't materialize would be its own instance of planning
   208→   against an assumption instead of a check. If OD-02's actual resolution turns out to need a `HUB-04`
   209→   interface change anyway, that's an ADR-gated change like any other (§2.1) — expensive, but not a scenario
   210→   this document failed to anticipate.
   211→
   212→2. **Deepen — per-blueprint floor, not a global lap floor.** Each blueprint's target floor is *its own
   213→   admission depth plus the number of laps completed since its own admission*, capped at depth 5 — not a
   214→   single floor number shared by every blueprint regardless of when it entered the matrix.
   215→
   216→**Why per-blueprint, not the global "lap 1→3, lap 2→4, lap 3→5" schedule stated in an earlier draft:** an
   217→external review correctly caught that the global version contradicts itself — a blueprint widened into the
   218→matrix during lap 2's widen step enters at depth 1, and lap 2's own deepen step would then require it to
   219→reach depth 4 in the same lap: three depth levels in one bet-cycle, alongside a full widen pass across all
   220→6 rings, for something that had zero prior laps to build in. The fix reviewed alongside that catch (exempt a
   221→blueprint from the floor for its admission lap only, deepening starts next lap) is directionally right but
   222→only *defers* the cliff by one lap rather than removing it — a blueprint admitted in lap 2 would still owe a
   223→4-level jump (1→5) by lap 3 under that version. The per-blueprint relative floor removes the cliff entirely:
   224→every blueprint deepens at the same steady +1-level-per-lap pace starting from its own admission, regardless
   225→of the global lap number, so nothing is ever asked to catch up faster than anything else did. Applied to the
   226→original 8: Milestone 0 gets them to depth 1–2 (their "lap 0"), lap 1 brings them to depth 3, lap 2 to depth
   227→4, lap 3 to depth 5 — which reproduces the original schedule's intent for the blueprints it was actually
   228→designed around, while extending consistently to every later admission instead of breaking on them.
   229→
   230→**Termination:** the lap structure ends when all 96 blueprints are at depth 5 and the matrix has fully
   231→widened — not at a fixed lap count, since how many laps that takes depends on how fast widening outpaces
   232→deepening, which isn't known until real laps run (see §5's throughput-unit caveat).
   233→
   234→This keeps the matrix genuinely two-dimensional throughout — width and depth grow together every cycle, at a
   235→uniform per-blueprint pace — rather than fully deepening a fixed subset before ever widening, or asking
   236→newly-widened blueprints to deepen faster than anything else did.
   237→
   238→**Gap A — lap ↔ bet relationship, stated:** a lap is not one atomic bet. It is one widen pass (up to 6
   239→ring-admissions) plus up to 6 deepening bets — one per ring that currently has a blueprint below its own
   240→per-blueprint floor (§4.2 scopes each such bet to a single named ring and target). Not every ring
   241→necessarily needs a deepening bet in every lap; a ring with nothing below floor that lap contributes only
   242→its widen-step admission.
   243→
   244→**Gap B — bet kill semantics, stated:** hitting the 1.5× ceiling (§4.2) means: stop the bet, keep whatever
   245→depth was actually reached — no forced revert, since the interface was already frozen at admission (§2.1), so
   246→partial deepening progress is never wasted. Re-scope explicitly: either the same ring gets the next
   247→deepening bet-slot with adjusted expectations, or the architecture lead defers that ring's remaining
   248→deepening to a later lap. This is a routine re-scoping event, not Milestone 0's "stop and reassess the whole
   249→model" — killing one bet doesn't imply the methodology itself is wrong, the way Milestone 0 exceeding its own
   250→ceiling would.
   251→
   252→**Gap C — is `INDEX.md` §5.2 actually queryable, checked against the live repo, not assumed:** yes, and more
   253→precisely than "yes/no" — it's a real Mermaid graph with genuine `A --> B` edges, but its current coverage is
   254→37 of 96 blueprints (all 20 Core, 10 Hub components explicitly marked "selected critical," `BRIDGE-01`, the 2
   255→exemplar Spokes `ISPOKE-01`/`ESPOKE-01`, and all 4 Deploy). `HUB-02` and `HUB-04` — this document's own named
   256→lap-1 widening candidates — are both in that covered set with real edges, so early laps have genuine data to
   257→widen against, not the arbitrary fallback gap C worried about. The real constraint: once widening needs a
   258→blueprint outside that 37-node coverage (most of `HUB-11`–`30` beyond the 10 already included, and every
   259→`ISPOKE`/`ESPOKE` beyond the 2 exemplars), `§5.2` has no edge data for it and the "next-most-depended-upon"
   260→rule has nothing to compute against. This isn't a lap-1 blocker — it's a known, scoped point where `§5.2`'s
   261→coverage needs extending, roughly once widening exhausts the 37-node set already covered. Tracked in §9, not
   262→treated as resolved.

   264→## 5. Calibration — formula, not a vibes-check
   265→
   266→```
   267→N  = blueprints in Milestone 0 = 8 (corrected — see §4)
   268→W  = weeks Milestone 0 actually took (measured, not estimated)
   269→throughput = N / W                    (blueprints/week)
   270→remaining  = 96 − N = 88 blueprints
   271→projected  = remaining / throughput = 11W   (weeks, bet-work only, excludes cooldowns)
   272→```
   273→
   274→Example: Milestone 0 takes 4 weeks → 44 weeks of remaining bet-work. Takes 6 weeks → 66 weeks.
   275→
   276→**Caveat this projection doesn't state on its own: `throughput` here is a *build* rate (depth 0→2, what
   277→Milestone 0 measures), applied to 88 blueprints that also need *deepening* passes (2→3→4→5), which is very
   278→likely a different unit, not just a smaller version of the same one.** Deepening could be faster than initial
   279→build — interfaces are already frozen (§2.1), patterns repeat within a ring — or slower, since depth 4–5 work
   280→(observability, hardening, security review) is often genuinely harder than getting a happy path working. The
   281→11W figure is therefore a **build-only estimate**, not a full project estimate, and should be read as such
   282→until real lap data (§4.3) produces a separate deepening throughput to add to it. This is tracked as an
   283→explicit unknown in §9, not resolved here by assertion.
   284→
   285→**On the "40–70 week" range stated in an earlier draft of this document: it was wrong, and re-deriving it
   286→properly matters, not just re-labeling it.** It was built on an uncorrected blueprint count, and a review of
   287→this document correctly flagged that the arithmetic didn't actually derive it from the stated formula.
   288→Re-running that same formula with the *correct* N=8 doesn't produce a lower, tighter number — it produces a
   289→*higher* one, because a smaller Milestone 0 measured over the same W implies lower throughput. At W=4–6:
   290→44–66 weeks of build-only bet-work (see caveat above), before cooldowns (§7, now 2 weeks each) and before
   291→Cooldown 0 (§3, now 2 weeks, fixed). Cooldown *count* isn't a number this document will manufacture — under
   292→the lap model, bet count isn't fixed in advance the way it was in the linear model, so a precise
   293→cooldown-overhead figure would be exactly the kind of false precision this section exists to avoid. Treat
   294→total elapsed time as **Milestone 0 (W) + Cooldown 0 (2wk) + build-work (11W) + deepening-work (unknown until
   295→lap 1) + cooldown overhead (unknown until a real bet cadence exists)** — not as a single number, and not until
   296→laps actually produce one.
   297→
   298→**Recalibrate throughput after every lap, not once.** Build throughput and deepening throughput are separate
   299→numbers (see caveat above), and within-ring pattern reuse will likely change both as the project goes on.
   300→Only the *current* projection, refreshed at every lap boundary, is ever trusted.
   301→
   302→**Until Milestone 0 completes and lap 1 establishes cadence:** no total is stated as fact anywhere in this
   303→document, including here.

   305→## 6. The linter is the second reviewer, not the notary
   306→
   307→With one engineer, `Verification/lint/run.php` is the only thing that catches what a second reviewer would
   308→have. Scope expands accordingly — and this expansion is itself real engineering work, budgeted explicitly
   309→rather than left to compete silently with blueprint deepening: **lint-scope expansion happens during cooldown
   310→weeks (§7), never during bet weeks.** If it doesn't fit in a cooldown, it carries to the next one rather than
   311→eating into bet time.
   312→
   313→**Not live during Milestone 0 itself.** Cooldown 0 (§3) is scoped to content contracts, not engineering lint
   314→— so Milestone 0 runs against the *existing* lint scope only. The expanded checks below become active
   315→starting with the first post-Milestone-0 cooldown. Don't assume the expanded scope is protecting Milestone 0's
   316→own build.
   317→
   318→| Check | Status |
   319→|---|---|
   320→| Cross-reference validity against `INDEX.md` (existing) | Already load-bearing |
   321→| Pulse 6-tuple consistency across repos | Promote from nice-to-have to required — this is now the only thing catching Structure-doc drift |
   322→| Naming drift, folder vs. `INDEX.md` (OD-05) | Promote from "docs cleanup" to lint-enforced, not manually tracked |
   323→| Soft-Freeze violations — a PR touching a frozen interface (§2.1) without a citing ADR | Auto-reject at CI, not a review comment |
   324→| Blueprint-fidelity drift — does the implementation still match its `STRUCTURE-XX` spec | **Honest caveat, not oversold:** full semantic diffing of code against prose spec isn't a solved CI problem. Start as a lighter-weight periodic check (a scripted structural diff — class/interface names present, method signatures match — not full behavioral equivalence) rather than claiming this is a complete automated gate from day one. Tighten it as patterns emerge from real drift instances. |
   325→
   326→The distinction on the last row matters: claiming an unimplemented capability is fully automated would be
   327→exactly the kind of unverified assertion this whole audit exists to catch, just relocated into the process
   328→document instead of the architecture docs.
   329→
   330→> **Reality check (verified against the live repo, 2026-08-10):** `run.php` in `Architecture/Verification/lint/`
   331→> performs exactly **three** checks — (1) reference targets exist / are in-range, (2) misattribution-phrase
   332→> guard, (3) structural completeness. It does **not** currently perform Pulse 6-tuple consistency, naming-drift,
   333→> soft-freeze, or blueprint-fidelity checks. Those rows above are the *intended* expansion target, not a
   334→> description of what the linter does today. See `DISCREPANCY-REGISTER.md` and `REPO-STATE-AUDIT.md`.

   336→## 7. Cooldowns: 2 weeks, not 1
   337→
   338→A 3-person team's 1-week cooldown gave each person ~2.3 days of catch-up. Solo, one week has to cover doc
   339→reconciliation, OD triage, refactor backlog, the review backlog that accumulated during the bet, *and*
   340→recovery time nobody else provides by covering for you. Solo burnout carries higher risk than team burnout
   341→precisely because there's no redundancy to absorb it. **Cooldowns are 2 weeks for the duration of solo
   342→operation.** Total timeline goes up; sustainability goes up more, and an unsustainable schedule that collapses
   343→at week 25 is a worse outcome than a longer one that actually completes.

   352→  out.
   353→- **Governance Rule 2** (no benchmark/timeline claim without a stated method) — this document's own §5 is the
   354→  SDLC-level application of the same rule that governs individual blueprint performance claims.

   356→### 8.1 OD sequencing under solo mode — two entries re-timed, not re-decided
   357→
   358→Solo, the cost of a wrong architectural call lands entirely on one person, with no second engineer to catch
   359→it in review. Two of the six open decisions need earlier resolution than the timing implied elsewhere:
   360→
   361→- **OD-06** (`ADR-010` opcache baseline, blocked on `CORE-02`) is mechanically forced to resolve **during
   362→  Milestone 0** — `CORE-02` is the first thing built there, so there's no later point where this stays
   363→  genuinely open without also blocking the milestone itself.
   364→- **OD-02** (post-quantum/algorithm-agility JWT signing) resolves **during Cooldown 1** (the first cooldown
   365→  after Milestone 0), not folded into Cooldown 0 and not read as a Milestone 0 completion gate. To be explicit
   366→  about a real tension an earlier draft left ambiguous: Milestone 0's success criterion (§4) is the Pulse round
   367→  trip working, full stop — OD-02 is not part of that criterion, and the round trip can and should succeed
   368→  before OD-02 is decided. Cooldown 0 is scoped tightly to content-facing contracts (§3) so marketer/media can
   369→  start on schedule; a deep crypto-architecture decision doesn't belong there without diluting what that phase
   370→  is for. `CORE-16`/`HUB-04` groundwork is already being scoped by Cooldown 1 regardless, which is the natural
   371→  point to close it out — earlier than the originally-planned "Bet 6," and unambiguously after Milestone 0's
   372→  own completion rather than entangled with it.
   373→
   374→OD-01, OD-03, OD-04, OD-05 keep their existing owners and timing from `OPEN-DECISIONS.md` — nothing about solo
   375→mode changes their urgency.

   377→## 9. Explicit unknowns — do not silently resolve these
   378→
   379→- The actual Milestone 0 duration, and therefore every downstream week-count.
   380→- Whether `CORE-02`'s real build time matches the "unblocks everything" framing it's had since Finding 8, or
   381→  turns out harder than expected as the first thing built solo.
   382→- Whether blueprint-fidelity drift-checking (§6) needs to become a harder automated gate sooner than planned,
   383→  based on how much real drift Milestone 0 and the first few deepening bets actually produce.
   384→- **Whether deepening throughput (depth 2→5) differs from build throughput (depth 0→2), and by how much (§5).**
   385→  Genuinely unresolvable until lap 1 (§4.3) produces a real deepening data point — the projection in §5 is
   386→  explicitly build-only until then.
   387→- **How many laps the widen/deepen structure (§4.3) actually takes to reach full coverage.** Depends on the
   388→  relative rate of widening vs. deepening, which is itself unknown until laps 1–2 run.
   389→- **`INDEX.md` §5.2's dependency-graph coverage (37 of 96 blueprints, verified) needs extending before widening
   390→  exhausts it.** Not a lap-1 blocker — `HUB-02`/`HUB-04` are both covered — but there's no stated trigger for
   391→  *when* to extend §5.2's coverage before the widen rule runs out of real edge data to compute against.
   392→  Trigger, disambiguated: extend a ring's §5.2 coverage once 50% **of that ring's currently-covered set** has
   393→  been admitted into the matrix (e.g., Hub's covered set is 10 of its 30 blueprints — extend once 5 of those 10
   394→  are admitted, not once 15 of all 30 are), since that's the point where the widen rule is about to run out of
   395→  real edges to choose from for that ring, which is the actual failure mode this trigger exists to prevent.
   396→
   397→Per Governance Rule 9 (this session's own precedent): open questions get recorded, never silently resolved. The
   398→six above go in `OPEN-DECISIONS.md` as new entries once this document is ratified.
